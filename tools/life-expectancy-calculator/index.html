How Does This Life Expectancy Calculator Work?

This free life expectancy calculator uses a baseline lifespan drawn from WHO global actuarial data — 71 years for males and 76 years for females — then applies scientifically validated adjustments for seven lifestyle factors. Each adjustment is sourced from peer-reviewed research published in The Lancet, JAMA, and the New England Journal of Medicine.

The calculator answers the question "how long will I live?" by combining your age, biological sex, smoking status, exercise frequency, sleep duration, stress level, and social connection quality into a single personalized estimate. It also shows your probability of reaching age 90 or 100, your days remaining, and an interactive slider so you can see how lifestyle changes affect your result in real time.

What Factors Affect Life Expectancy Most?

Research from the Million Veteran Program (2023) found that lifestyle factors account for 70–80% of lifespan variation — meaning your daily habits matter far more than genetics for most people. The most accurate life expectancy calculators incorporate all of these variables:

  • Smoking: Heavy daily smokers lose an average of 8–10 years. Quitting before age 40 recovers nearly all of that loss (CDC, 2023).
  • Exercise: Exercising 5+ times per week reduces all-cause mortality risk by up to 50% and adds 3–4.5 years on average.
  • Sleep: A 2025 OHSU study found sleep quality has a stronger link to longevity than diet or exercise. The optimal range is 7–9 hours per night.
  • Chronic stress: Persistent high stress shortens lifespan by up to 2.3 years through chronic inflammation pathways.
  • Social connections: Strong social bonds add up to 3 years. Loneliness carries the same mortality risk as smoking 15 cigarettes per day (Holt-Lunstad, 2015).

How to Increase Life Expectancy — Science-Backed Methods

You can meaningfully increase your life expectancy by targeting the highest-impact lifestyle factors. Combined, these changes can add 15 or more years to your estimated lifespan:

  • Quit smoking (+5 to +10 years): Quitting at any age reduces mortality risk. Quitting before 40 recovers nearly all lost years.
  • Exercise 5x per week (+3 to +4.5 years): 30 minutes of brisk walking five days a week cuts all-cause mortality risk by 50%.
  • Sleep 7–9 hours nightly (+2 to +3 years): Consistently sleeping in the optimal range is now linked to stronger longevity effects than diet alone.
  • Manage chronic stress (+1.5 to +2.3 years): Daily mindfulness practice, regular social interaction, and adequate rest all reduce cortisol-driven inflammation.
  • Strengthen social bonds (+1 to +3 years): Even one close friendship reduces mortality risk significantly.
  • Maintain a healthy BMI (+1 to +3 years): Obesity increases mortality risk for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and certain cancers.

Use the interactive selectors in your calculator result to see exactly how each change affects your personal life expectancy estimate.

The average global life expectancy is 73 years (WHO, 2024). Men average 71 years and women average 76 years worldwide. However, your personal lifespan depends heavily on lifestyle factors including smoking, exercise, sleep, stress, and social connections — which can add or subtract 15+ years from that baseline. How many years do I have left?+
To calculate how many years you have left, subtract your current age from your estimated life expectancy. For example, if you are 35 years old and your life expectancy is 82 years, you have approximately 47 years remaining — or about 17,155 days.
